PALAWAN STATE UNIVERSITY
Office of the University President
Puerto Princesa City

MEMORANDUM
Series of 2026

TO: The University Community (Students, Faculty, Staff, and Visitors) in the Main and Manalo Campuses in Puerto Princesa City
Subject: Revised Campus Access Policy
Date: 23 March 2026

1. Rationale

In response to reports of theft and unauthorized entries, the University is implementing these heightened measures to ensure the protection of our constituents and the orderly management of campus access.

2. Implementation Dates

The pilot implementation period has concluded. This revised policy, incorporating evaluated community feedback, is effective as of March 23, 2026.

3. Vehicle Entry Protocols

A. University Employees and Students

All vehicles should display the latest PSU gate pass stickers.

B. Gate Access & Flow

To optimize fuel efficiency, vehicles are authorized to use both the main and back gates for entry and exit, choosing the gate nearest to their destination. However, in exceptional circumstances—such as accidents, special events, or heavy congestion—the Chief Security Officer, in consultation with the Chief Administrative Officer, is authorized to temporarily suspend two-way traffic. This suspension shall remain in effect only until normal traffic flow is restored.

C. Entrance and Exit Procedures

  1. Present to the guard on duty a valid driver's license or/and a government-issued ID of the driver.
  2. Surrender a valid government-issued ID (except the driver's license) to the guard for the duration of the visit.
  3. Passengers who are not affiliated with the University must submit a valid government-issued ID so that they can be issued a Visitor's Pass (Item 3.C.5 hereof).
  4. Visitors and/or vehicles with a previously Approved Campus Access signed by any of the following shall be allowed entry only during the day/s and time provided in their request:
    1. University President or Officer-in-Charge;
    2. Vice President;
    3. Or any other designated official of the University.
  5. All visitors (both pedestrians or passengers) are required to obtain a signature from the visited office prior to departure. The Visitor's Pass shall contain a Control Number issued by the Security Guard on duty, along with the visitor's name, address, contact number, whom they are meeting, purpose, time in and time out, and signatures of both the visitor and the contact person.
  6. All IDs will be returned upon the surrender of a fully completed and signed Visitor's Pass.

D. Sticker Availability

The GSO must ensure a sufficient supply of stickers for all eligible personnel.

E. Tiered Enforcement for Missing Stickers

  1. 1st Offense: Verify status via ID and issue a Temporary Vehicle Gate Pass (Collateral ID system) good for 5 days to allow him/her to secure a regular vehicle pass.
  2. 2nd Offense: Denial of Entry until the sticker requirement is met.

4. Pedestrian Entry Protocols

Visitors entering through the pedestrian gates must:

  1. Present a valid (Section 6.C hereof) photo ID to the Guard-on-Duty.
  2. Surrender said ID in exchange for a Visitor's Pass/ID. PSU retirees shall be allowed to use their former PSU ID.
  3. The Visitor's Pass must be signed by the personnel or officer of the office visited. IDs will be returned upon surrender of the signed pass.

5. Transport & Delivery Services

A. Motor Taxis (Maxim, Angkas, etc.)

Backride vehicles are permitted entry to drop off passengers at the parking area in front of the Student Innovation Center. Drivers must present an ID at the gate.

B. Tricycles

PUVs with stickers may enter freely; those without must surrender an ID. Drivers must stay with vehicles (max 15 mins) and engines off.

C. Courier & Delivery Riders

Riders must proceed directly to the designated drop-off point closest to the recipient. At the PSU main campus:

  1. Zone A: Vacant space fronting the Student Innovation Building.
  2. Zone B: Vacant space Supply Storage Room (near the CBA).
  3. Parcel Policy: Security guards are strictly prohibited from receiving or storing parcels on behalf of students or staff.

D. Inclusivity Exemptions

PWDs, seniors, and medical emergencies, including VIP guests, are exempt from restricted drop-off zones; vehicles may take them directly to their destination building and offices through door-to-door delivery.

6. General Security & Conduct

A. Right to Inspect

PSU reserves the right to search bags, packages, vehicles (visual search only), and other items brought into the campus.

The following are strictly prohibited inside campus:

  1. Firearms and deadly weapons, except those falling under Item 6.B hereof (Firearms and Deadly Weapons — Exceptions and Protocols).
  2. Illegal drugs and prohibited substances.
  3. Explosives and hazardous materials.
  4. Alcoholic beverages (unless authorized by administration).
  5. Any item deemed dangerous by security personnel.

Confiscation and proper reporting shall be immediately implemented.

Exit Procedures:

  1. All bags and carried items may be subject to inspection.
  2. Gate pass is required for bringing out university property. Only the property custodian and supply officer shall be allowed to release Gate Pass for this purpose.
  3. Security personnel may verify documents before allowing exit.

B. Firearms and Deadly Weapons (Exceptions and Protocols)

While the University is a generally designated "Gun-Free Zone," the following exceptions apply strictly to authorized law enforcement officers on duty, bank security escorts, qualified licensed holders, and University security personnel within the university community:

a. Special Exception for Licensed Holders

Authorized employees and students who possess firearms for personal protection must adhere to the following mandatory registration protocol:

  1. All firearms must be legally registered with a valid License to Own and Possess Firearms (LTOPF).
  2. Possess a valid Permit to Carry Firearms Outside of Residence (PTCFOR).
  3. The firearm must be officially registered with the University Security Office. This ensures that the administration is aware of which employees or students are carrying firearms within the university premises for safety and monitoring purposes.

b. Law Enforcement Personnel

Uniformed law enforcement officers (PNP, AFP, NBI, etc.) on official duty are permitted to carry their service firearms provided they present their mission orders upon request by the Guard-on-Duty or prior approved access request. In the absence of any of these, the enforcement officer shall be required to surrender his/her firearm with the guard on duty.

c. Bank Escort Security

Security personnel who are escorting bank personnel who are replenishing cash in ATMs within the campus shall be allowed to carry their firearms within the campus during the transaction.

d. University Security Personnel

University security personnel who are on duty shall be allowed to carry their service firearms within the campus.

C. Acceptable IDs

All visitors, delivery couriers, and individuals without a valid PSU ID are strongly encouraged to present a valid government-issued ID. These IDs are preferred due to their security features and verifiable nature. The list of acceptable IDs (primary and secondary) are as follows:

a. Primary IDs:

  • Driver's License
  • Passport
  • Professional Identification Card (PRC)
  • PhilSys (National ID)
  • UMID / SSS / GSIS ID
  • Senior Citizen / PWD ID
  • Voter's ID / Certification
  • Postal ID (Digital or Physical)
  • NBI Clearance (Original)

b. Secondary:

  • Company ID
  • Valid Student ID (from other Universities)
  • Integrated Bar of the Philippines (IBP) ID

c. For PSU retirees, their former PSU IDs.

D. Mandatory Visibility and Facial Identification

To ensure that all individuals entering the campus can be accurately identified by security personnel and captured by CCTV systems, the following rules apply to all vehicles (motorcycles and cars) at the point of entry and while within the campus:

  1. Removal of Helmets (upon entry): All motorcycle riders and their passengers are strictly required to remove their helmets before approaching the security checkpoint.
  2. Removal of Face Coverings: Drivers and passengers wearing balaclavas, full-face masks, heavy tinted visors, or hoodies that obscure the face must remove or lower them for visual verification by the Guard-on-Duty.
  3. Vehicle Interiors: Drivers of four-wheeled vehicles with heavy tint are required to roll down their windows (front and passenger sides) to allow security to verify the number of occupants and their identities.
  4. Refusal of Entry: Any individual who refuses to comply with facial identification protocols will be denied entry to the University premises.

E. Visit Verification

To ensure campus security, Guards-on-Duty may verify a visitor's legitimacy with the concerned PSU office before granting entry. Verification may be conducted via:

  1. Radio communication with the relevant personnel or official.
  2. Phone or text using the gate's official mobile phone or the visitor's device (if available).
  3. Official documentation, such as a printed invitation or correspondence.
  4. Personal escort by a university employee or student.
  5. Other practical means deemed necessary to confirm the visit.

F. Serving Warrants to Personnel and Students

Serving warrants of arrest within a university campus shall be governed by protocols designed to balance law enforcement duties with academic freedom, privacy, and the safety of the campus community. Serving warrants on campus shall be governed by the following:

  1. Initial Contact: Law enforcement agents are directed to contact the university's Chief Security Officer (CSO) (warrants for personnel) or the Office of Student Affairs and Services (warrants for students) first.
  2. Coordination: CSO accompanies law enforcement to the location of the person named in the warrant.
  3. Minimizing Disruption: The arrest should cause the least disruption possible to university activities.
  4. Secluded Service: Warrants must be served in a private or secluded area rather than in a classroom or public space.
  5. Verification: CSO shall verify the existence of an active warrant before assisting outside agencies.

G. Dress Code

All visitors must wear decent and appropriate attire. Entry may be denied for attire deemed inappropriate for an academic environment.

H. Security Monitoring

The University, through its security personnel, reserves the right to approach, interview, and report any individuals exhibiting suspicious behavior within campus grounds.

I. Adherence to the Safe Spaces Act in public transportation contexts

In compliance with Republic Act No. 11313 (The Safe Spaces Act), the following protocols shall apply to ensure the safety and dignity of all students and employees:

  1. Zero Tolerance for Harassment: Any form of gender-based sexual harassment, discrimination, or abuse—including but not limited to catcalling, wolf-whistling, persistent uninvited comments, or any physical or verbal act that creates a hostile environment—committed by PUV drivers or operators is strictly condemned.
  2. Due Process and Reporting: Any student or employee who encounters a violation from a PUV driver while inside the campus is encouraged to report the incident to the Office of Gender and Development, including the time of incident, description of the vehicle, and the driver's name, if available. This may be done using the S.A.F.E.R. Tool (Safe Access & Fair Enforcement Reporting) — a QR-based messenger reporting app embedded in the PROTECT app (Item 13 hereof) system implemented by the University. Once the complaint is formalized, it shall be officially endorsed to the Committee on Decorum and Investigation (CODI) for proper hearing and administrative review.
  3. Support Services: The University, through the Office of Gender and Development, shall provide immediate psychosocial support and guidance to the complainant to ensure their well-being throughout the reporting and legal process.

7. Notification Protocol for University Events

To ensure the smooth entry of external guests for official functions, the following coordination protocol must be followed:

  1. Advance Notice: Event organizers or host offices must submit a formal notice or approved activity permit to the Security Office (Guard House) at least forty-eight (48) hours prior to the event.
  2. Guest List Submission: The notice must include a comprehensive list of names of external guests, the nature of the event, the specific venue, and the expected time of arrival.
  3. Special Access: For high-profile events or large delegations, organizers may request a "Guest Lane" to expedite entry, provided that guest names are pre-verified against the submitted list.
  4. Walk-in Guests: External guests not included in a pre-submitted list will be treated as infrequent visitors and must follow the standard ID surrender and logging procedure.

8. Gate Pass Applications of Visitors with Vehicles

To avoid entry delays, students and personnel are encouraged to obtain the latest gate passes from the designated guard on duty at the Main Gate immediately.

9. 24/7 Heightened Security Mandate

To ensure a proactive defense against theft and unauthorized entry, the University is shifting from stationary monitoring to a dynamic, 24-hour surveillance model. Security operations are now categorized into intensified Daytime Visibility and Nighttime Interdiction.

10. Fixed Post Integrity

At all times, the Main Gate and Back Gate must remain manned by stationed security personnel. Under no circumstances shall these primary access points be left unattended. These officers are responsible for strict ID verification and vehicle inspections as provided in this Memorandum.

11. Roving Inspection Protocols

A. Daytime Operations (6:00 AM – 6:00 PM)

  1. Presence Patrols: Roving guards are directed to maintain high visibility in student hubs, corridors, and parking areas.
  2. Suspicious Character Monitoring: Personnel shall actively monitor and approach individuals who loiter in non-public areas or exhibit behavior inconsistent with academic activity.
  3. Building Checks: Conduct random walk-throughs of college buildings and admin offices to ensure only authorized personnel and students with visible IDs are present.

B. Nighttime Operations (6:00 PM – 6:00 AM)

  1. Mandatory Hourly Sweeps: Roving teams must conduct comprehensive perimeter and interior inspections every sixty (60) minutes.
  2. Target Zones: Focus shall be placed on the laboratory complexes, administrative offices, and dimly lit perimeter boundaries.
  3. Utility & Breach Audit: During night sweeps, guards must check for unsecured windows, unbolted doors, and malfunctioning perimeter lights.

12. Reporting, Security Personnel Conduct, and Accountability

  1. Security Logbook: Every roving cycle must be logged, indicating the specific areas checked and the status of the "all-clear." Any irregularities must be reported to the Chief Security Officer immediately.
  2. Direct Interdiction: Security personnel are empowered to interview and, if necessary, escort unauthorized or suspicious-looking individuals off the premises.
  3. Communication: Constant radio contact between roving units and gate-stationed personnel is mandatory to ensure rapid response coordination.
  4. Professionalism: Security must undergo "Customer Service & Inclusivity" training, as necessary. The CSO shall submit daily security reports and consolidated monthly findings for policy development.

13. Incident Reporting through the P.R.O.T.E.C.T. System

(PSU Real-time Online Threat and Emergency Communications Tool)

The community is encouraged to assist our security force.

Scan the QR code posted at the guard house to submit a real-time report. Please include:

  1. Photos of the incident or individual.
  2. Exact Location within the campus.
  3. Detailed Description of the observed activity and perpetrators.
  4. Email address of the reporting party (for issuance of updates).

14. SOP for the P.R.O.T.E.C.T. Online Reporting System

To ensure a streamlined response to campus threats, the following protocol shall be adopted:

Phase 1: Incident Trigger & Deployment

  1. Initial Report: A client or personnel submits a report of a suspicious individual or incident via the P.R.O.T.E.C.T. system (Chat Group/Web Link).
  2. Rapid Dispatch: Upon receipt of the alert, the Security Office shall immediately deploy the nearest roving personnel to the specified area for investigation and intervention.

Phase 2: Documentation & Case Management

  1. Security Reporting & Ticketing: The Security Office shall accomplish the Official Incident Report Form.
  2. New Requirement: The report must now include the reporting party's full name and email address for verification and follow-up.
  3. Ticketing: Every incident must be assigned a unique Case Ticket Number (e.g., PSU-2026-001) for tracking purposes.
  4. Database Integration: All report details, including timestamps and photographic evidence, must be uploaded/synced to the PSU Central Security Database for long-term data analysis and pattern recognition.

Phase 3: Queue Management & Data Privacy

  1. Group De-cluttering: To maintain the technical capacity of the reporting group (PROTECT) and ensure data privacy, the reporting client/personnel shall be removed from the active Chat Group once their initial statement and evidence have been successfully captured and logged.

Phase 4: Resolution & Feedback Loop

  1. Case Closure: Once the investigation is concluded or the threat is neutralized, the Case Ticket shall be marked as "CLOSED."
  2. Final Notification: A formal resolution update shall be sent to the reporting party via their registered email address to ensure transparency and encourage future civic cooperation.

15. Security Personnel Duties and Responsibilities

  1. Strictly enforce these policies, rules and regulations.
  2. Inspect offices for compliance with austerity policy of the University and report observed violations.
  3. Maintain professional conduct at all times.
  4. Immediately report suspicious activities.
  5. Implement measures to protect the University personnel, students, properties and projects.
  6. Secure premises and personnel by patrolling campus perimeters.
  7. Guard restricted areas against unlawful entry.
  8. Control the entrance and movements of pedestrians and vehicles.
  9. Prevent and investigate loss or damage of the University and personnel properties.
  10. Carry out special assignments during periods of emergency.
  11. Coordinate with University Administration and local authorities when necessary.
  12. Ensure proper turnover of posts during shifting.
  13. Wear the complete designated uniform while on duty.
  14. Perform other related functions that may be assigned by the Head of the General Services Office, Chief Admin Officer, VP for Finance and Administration, and by the Office of University President.

Additional roles/duties and functions of the Security personnel:

  1. Must show utmost courtesy and respect to all individuals at all times.
  2. Must remain unruffled even under pressure and stay calm at all times under all conditions.
  3. Shall not be involved in any illegal transactions or acts for the entire duration of the contract.
  4. Roving guards during ocular inspections must turn off all lights and office equipment when not in use. Street lights must be turned off at 5:30 AM and turned on at 6:00 PM.
  5. Guards shall extend assistance to the elderly and other persons or students in need, provided their post is not abandoned.
  6. Shall help maintain campus cleanliness by calling the attention of any persons littering inside the premises.
  7. Shall, through the Chief Security Officer, submit a daily security and accomplishment report to the GSO, copy furnished the CAO, VPFA, and the Office of the President via email.
  8. Shall act promptly and decisively during emergency situations.
  9. Shall assist pupils and students in crossing the streets when necessary; and
  10. Perform other functions as may be deemed necessary.

16. Violations

Non-compliance with this policy shall result in:

  1. Denial of entry
  2. Confiscation of unauthorized items (Item 6.A hereof)
  3. Administrative sanctions, subject to the required due process
  4. Filing of appropriate legal action if necessary, subject to the required due process

17. Effectivity

This policy shall take effect immediately upon approval by the Office of the University President, without prejudice to subsequent amendment or revocation.

All previously issued memoranda, orders, guidelines, or parts thereof that are inconsistent with the provisions of this Memorandum are hereby repealed, amended, or modified accordingly.

For information dissemination and guidance of all concerned.

MARISSA S. PONTILLAS
OIC, Office of the President
